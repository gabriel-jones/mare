# Playable foundation

Serve the parent `lunar-art-test/` directory: `python3 -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1`, then open http://127.0.0.1:8765/game/.

Dependencies are installed in the parent directory (`npm install` there). Original art study remains at `/`.

## Play

- WASD: screen-relative movement with brisk acceleration and stopping. Shift: run. Space: lunar-gravity jump. Scroll: zoom.
- B opens the Build menu; click a card to pick it. Only constant tools have letters: C belt, H habitat, M mining zone, G robots panel, O orbit, Z dismantle (press again to deselect). Number keys 1–9 and 0 select inventory slots; F eats. R rotates the footprint in four directions; ground arrows show machine ports (cyan in, amber out). Click ground to place. With the conveyor, drag a route: it follows the pointer around corners, backtracking trims it, and dragging over existing belts turns them to match (click one belt to point it the current way). Escape dismisses placement.
- Walk within 5 m and hold E to construct. Blueprints turn red while you stand where the finished structure would trap you. Belts are walkable.
- To take things down, choose Build → Tools → Dismantle (Z). Click a structure or blueprint, or drag across several, for a full refund. Fittings come off before the floor beneath them. The red highlight shows what will go; if something can't be removed yet, the reason is shown instead. Pressing Delete or Backspace while pointing at a structure also works.
- Production: build a hopper, then mark a Mining zone (M; up to four, each numbered on the ground). Rovers dig, take regolith to the nearest hopper and ice straight into an ice melter, and recharge while docked. Robots (G) lists each rover with its job and charge, assigns it to a zone or Auto (nearest zone with work), and removes zones. Belt the hopper's output into a processor's input, and its output into a storage crate. Press E beside a crate to collect metal and silicon. Oxygen goes to the colony reserve.
- Rooms: drag to paint habitat (H) or greenhouse squares; touching squares join into one building with solid walls. Add an airlock against the room, arrow facing out; it is the only way in. Inside, the whole building's roof lifts away. Rooms fill from the oxygen reserve and need power to stay pressurised.
- Food: put grow beds in a greenhouse, then press E on a ripe bed to harvest potatoes. Load two into a galley in a habitat with E, and take the meal with E. A water recycler tops up water. Eat with F when the Food meter runs low; hunger slows you but never kills.
- Ship AI: the panel at top left gives the next task; a cyan beam and a chevron at your feet point the way. The processor stays locked until you hold E at the old lander in the crater to the east, then press E at the Starship to install its core. The crater's shadowed floor has ice: build an ice melter and mark a mining zone on the ice; the rover delivers ice straight into the melter, which fills the water reserve.
- Space (Build → Space): a fabricator turns metal and silicon into satellite parts (E switches between the satellite kit, which makes one satellite's worth in turn, and a single part). A satellite assembler joins one frame, two solar wings and two compute chips into a satellite. A robot dock builds haulers (E; 25 metal, 10 silicon). Haulers fetch materials, parts and satellites from crates and machine outputs and deliver them where they're needed, satellites to the Starship first, and recharge at their dock. An electrolyser splits water into hydrolox (it always leaves 20 water for the colony) and fills propellant tanks, which must touch the landing pad and pump into the ship. With 80 fuel and a satellite aboard (a hauler loads it, or carry one from the assembler and press E at the ship), hold E beside the Starship to launch. The ship climbs to orbit, deploys its satellites and waits there.
- Orbit (O, or the button at top right): a 3D view of the Moon with every satellite on its own ring. Drag to turn it and scroll to zoom. Choose research here; each satellite earns 4 points a minute. Return to the pad sends the Starship home. The first technology unlocks the rover bay, which builds extra mining rovers (E; up to four).
- Machine status: each machine has a lamp (steady green fine, amber pulse low, orange blink fault, grey idle); batteries show charge cells and the hopper and melter a fill gauge. A small glyph bubble floats over anything that needs you (no power, no ice, no water, or a warning triangle), a green bolt over charging batteries and a cyan wrench over blueprints; charging and blueprints fill a ring as they progress. Machines light their surroundings at night.
- `?sandbox=1` plays a throwaway colony saved separately from your real one.
- Save/Load and 20-second autosave use browser storage. New colony requires inline confirmation. No server-side save or co-op yet.
- Time×12 speeds the day/night and power simulation for testing; normal cycle is 240 seconds. Recipes and timings are provisional.

## Development

`npm test` in this folder runs simulation/controller tests with Node's built-in runner. Shared interfaces are in `CONTRACT.md`. Rendering is separate from JSON simulation state. `main.js` handles input and integration; `sim.js` handles construction/power/saves; `controller.js` handles movement/collision; `world.js` handles Three.js presentation; `ui.js` and `game.css` handle HUD.

Blender-authored models and the rigged astronaut are in `assets/`; the character uses Idle, Walk, Run, Jump, and Build clips with blended transitions. Model studio: http://127.0.0.1:8765/game/art-lab.html. Scripts live in `blender/`. Repeated structures have merged static geometry by material.

Production: `defs.js` holds structure definitions, footprints and rotated ports; `production.js` steps the rover, belts, hoppers, processors and crates; `paths.js` is the rover's grid A*; `production-view.js` renders the rover, instanced belt items and the mining overlay. `production.test.mjs` covers the full loop. Production models are built by `blender/build_production.py`.

Rooms: `habitat.js` derives joined rooms, one-metre wall segments, airlocks, wall collision and the "would this shut the player in" check from painted room squares (shared by controller, simulation and rendering); `habitat-view.js` assembles each room from the Blender kit (`blender/build_modular.py`), merges it by material and draws each unfinished square on its own. Lamps and room ceilings share a fixed pool of six point lights, so placing them never recompiles shaders. Review page: `/game/art-direction/habitat-preview.html` (`?inside=1`, `?night=1`). `habitat.test.mjs` covers rooms, airlocks, fittings and collision.

Life support: `life.js` steps pressurisation, grow beds, the galley, the recycler and hunger, and handles eating and E interactions; `inventory.js` is the ten-slot carried inventory. Grow bed, galley and recycler models are built by the same Blender script; their `Plants` and `MealTray` nodes are animated at runtime. `life.test.mjs` covers the loop and the version 2 save migration.

Expedition: `mission.js` holds the ship AI's objectives (eight for the crater, six more for the first launch), the processor lock, salvage and install; `watcher.js` decides when the rim figure appears and slips away (never saved); `expedition-view.js` draws the lander, tracks, ice, waypoint and figure. The lander and ice melter are built by `blender/build_expedition.py`. `mission.test.mjs` covers the objective flow, the ice-to-water chain, the watcher and the version 3 migration.

Space: `space.js` steps electrolysers, propellant tanks, the Starship (pad, ascent, orbit, descent), research and robot building; `hauler.js` matches haulers to jobs and drives them with the rover's pathing; fabricator and assembler recipes live in `production.js`. `orbit-view.js` is the orbital view with its own renderer, drawn only while open. Models are built by `blender/build_space.py`. Review page: `/game/art-direction/space-preview.html` (`?launch=1`, `?orbit=1`). `space.test.mjs` covers parts, hauling, fuelling, launch, research and the rover bay.

Current limits: shared electrical grid, one technology to research, no orbital mechanics beyond the visual. No combat or multiplayer yet. Those are subsequent implementation phases, described in the root planning documents.

Terrain: `terrain.js` owns deterministic basin height, craters, cave landmarks and boulders; `terrain-view.js` renders the surface. New construction rejects slopes over 0.2 gradient, foundation height differences over 0.38 m and intersecting large rocks. Movement blocks slopes above 0.65 and solid boulders. Cave mouths now lead to explorable procedural passages; alien behavior is not implemented yet. Legacy saves bypass new terrain placement validation to preserve colonies.

Caves: `caves.js` generates connected seeded networks and headlamp line of sight; `cave-view.js` renders discovered floors and walls with instancing; `cave-space.js` and `travel.js` preserve world position, facing and screen movement across entrance thresholds. The terrain shader fades the roof along the same camera view rather than switching scenes or zoom. `player.cave` and `exploredCaves` are optional, backward-compatible save fields. Run speed is always 6.5 m/s.

Current terrain direction: open, low-poly / voxel-inspired lunar plains. Four-metre shelves, beveled terrace boundaries, local impact craters and separate cave-bearing hills replace the enclosed crater. Concept references and exact generation prompts are in `art-direction/`. Blender source is `assets/lunar-plains-kit.blend`; authored rock variants are instanced in `terrain-view.js`. Terrain collision and entrance regression coverage remain in `sim.test.mjs`.
