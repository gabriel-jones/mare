# Implementation plan

Phase A implemented: solo playable foundation — isometric WASD, seeded terrain, grid blueprints, material spending, manual construction, solar/battery lighting, enterable fixed-footprint habitat, save/load.

Acceptance: move and jump, place/rotate/cancel valid footprints, reject invalid/overlapping placements, hold E to construct in range, observe night lights and battery accounting, reload saved world. Keep art viewer intact.

Phase B implemented: mining and production — the starting mining rover, designated mining areas with finite regolith, hoppers, conveyor belts with designated machine ports, a regolith processor (metal, silicon, oxygen), storage crates the player collects from, and dismantling.

Acceptance: designate a mining area; the rover paths around structures, digs, depletes cells, returns to the nearest hopper, unloads and recharges; the hopper feeds a belt only from its output port; belts keep spacing, chain, queue when blocked and refuse belts pointing back; the processor accepts regolith only at its input port, stops without power and when its output is blocked; refined goods reach a crate and E collects them; saves round-trip mid-production and version 1 colonies migrate with a starter rover.

Phase C implemented: rooms, food and life support — rooms painted square by square (habitat, greenhouse) joining into one building, airlock squares as the only way in, pressurisation from the oxygen reserve with power and a clear airlock, grow beds, a galley, a water recycler with a starting water stopgap (ice arrives in D), hunger, and a ten-slot carried inventory.

Acceptance: drag-paint squares that join habitats and greenhouses with no wall between them; airlocks must back onto a room and open onto clear ground, and nothing solid or roofed may cover an airlock entrance; a room cannot be finished around the player until it has a way out; rooms fill from the oxygen reserve and pressurise only when powered with a clear airlock; grow beds (greenhouse only) drink water and power to ripen potatoes that E harvests; the galley (habitat only) cooks two potatoes into a meal; the recycler reclaims water; satiety drains over ten minutes, slows walking and building when low, never kills, and raw potatoes or meals restore it; carried goods stack in ten slots and full pockets refuse refunds; version 2 colonies migrate habitat modules to painted squares with an airlock.

Phase D implemented: the crater expedition — a ship AI objective panel that walks a new colony through power, shelter and mining, then sends the player to a derelict robotic lander in a crater east of the base. The processor is locked until its core is salvaged (hold E at the lander) and installed (E at the Starship). The crater's shadowed floor holds water ice that the rover mines into a hopper; belts carry it to a new ice melter that fills the water reserve. Unsettling details only: three-toed tracks leading from the lander over the rim, pried-open panels, and a figure on the rim that slips away when approached or looked toward.

Acceptance: a new colony's objectives advance in order (and skip ahead when work is already done) with a waypoint beam and a direction marker; the processor shows as locked with the reason and refuses placement; holding E for three seconds at the lander yields the core (refused with full pockets) and E at the ship installs it and unlocks processors; a mining area touching ice mines only ice, the rover never mixes loads, and a belted, powered melter turns ice into water up to the cap; the rim figure appears only off the player's line of sight, never acts and vanishes; saves round-trip the mission at version 4, and older colonies count as already repaired.

Since D: status glyph bubbles (no text) with progress rings, work lights retired (every machine lights its surroundings), B opens the build menu, and multiple mining zones with rover assignment in a Robots panel (G); rovers carry ice straight into melters (save version 5).

Phase E implemented: satellites, launch and research. A fabricator makes satellite frames, solar wings and compute chips; an assembler builds satellites. A robot dock builds haulers that carry materials, parts and satellites between crates, machines and the Starship. An electrolyser turns water into hydrolox for propellant tanks beside the pad, which fuel the ship. Holding E at the ship launches it with an animated liftoff; it deploys satellites in orbit and returns on command. A 3D orbital view (O) shows the Moon, satellite rings and the ship, and hosts research: satellites earn points that unlock the rover bay, which builds more mining rovers. Six new ship AI objectives guide it.

Acceptance: the kit fabricator makes one satellite's parts in turn and stops when full or unfed; the assembler needs one frame, two wings and two chips; haulers prefer the ship, then the assembler, then fabricators, never double up on a delivery, recharge at their dock and replan after loading; electrolysers keep 20 water back and stop when tanks are full; tanks only fuel the ship when they touch the pad; launch needs 80 fuel and a satellite, ascent takes 9 s, orbit deploys the payload and empties the tanks, and Return brings the ship down in 10 s; research accrues per satellite and unlocks the rover bay, which builds up to four rovers; saves round-trip at version 6 and version 5 colonies load with an empty ship.

Local play: http://127.0.0.1:8765/game/ (`?sandbox=1` plays a throwaway colony on a separate save). Controls: WASD, Space, B opens the build menu (C belt, H habitat, M mining zone, Z dismantle keep letters; press again to deselect), G robots, O orbit and research, 1–9 and 0 select inventory slots, F eats (the selected food, else the best food carried), R rotates, click places, drag paints room squares or lays a belt route that can turn corners (and redirects belts it crosses), Escape dismisses selection, E held builds within 5m and pressed beside a ripe grow bed, galley or crate uses it, held at the crater lander salvages its core and pressed at the Starship installs it, pressed at a fabricator switches its recipe, at the assembler takes a satellite, at a dock or rover bay builds a robot, at the Starship loads a carried satellite, and held there launches; the Dismantle tool (Build → Tools, or Z) removes whatever is clicked or dragged over, fittings before floors, for a full refund, with a red highlight and the reason when something can't come down yet; Delete or Backspace does the same for the structure you're pointing at. Scroll zooms. Autosave every 20s and on page exit. Time×12 accelerates power/daylight/production/life support, not movement.

Verification: 54 automated simulation/controller/production/habitat/life-support/export checks pass, plus the real backed-up v2 save migrates and round-trips. Browser-verified (sandbox and isolated preview): drag painting with edge-connected strokes, greenhouse glazing and joined roof cut-away, airlock entry, eating, harvest, galley cooking and collection, hotbar and build palette. No multiplayer or full colony-load benchmarks yet.

Phase E+ implemented: the orbital view is a procedural solar system (Sun, eight planets, the Moon, the main belt around Ceres) with GPU-baked surfaces and fly-to navigation between the system and each body.

Phase F planned: the caves become a place worth going.
- F1 Seamless entry. Build each cave's view and compile its shaders while the player walks up to the mouth (within ~30 m), keep it after leaving, so crossing the threshold costs nothing.
- F2 Horror sight. A dim ~14 m circle of visibility around the player that fades to black, plus a brighter headlamp beam ahead. Explored areas outside sight stay as faint cold outlines. Drifting dust in the beam, a vignette and colour drain that deepen with depth, the headlamp stuttering near artifacts and aliens, and shapes glimpsed at the edge of the dark that vanish when lit.
- F3 Deeper zones. The three entrances keep their seeded first zone; each leads down a shaft to two deeper zones that are larger, darker and more dangerous. Floors stay flat; interest comes from layout and props. Done: zones sit 200 m below, linked by a ladder pit down and a lit shaft up; the next zone is built while the player nears a shaft.
- F4 Terrain features. Rock-spire and stalagmite clusters that split rooms into paths; chasms crossed only on narrow rock bridges; glowing crystal veins and geodes that light their surroundings; frozen ice pools; alien ruins (carved pillars, doorways, glyphs); hazards (gas vents, unstable floor, toxic pools) that hurt the player. Done: spires, chasms with bridges, crystals, ice, ruins with glyphs, gas vents (visual only until F6); unstable floor and toxic pools are left for F6.
- F5 Reasons to go. Rare minerals (titanium ore, rare-earth crystal) found only underground and required by mid and late buildings; alien artifacts that feed research; blueprints found in ruins that unlock buildings; story clues about the tracks and the watcher. The surface carries the early game; mid and late tech needs the caves. Everything is carried out by hand.
- F6 Risk. Player health (hazards now, aliens in G). At zero the player wakes in the habitat and what they carried lies where they fell. Flares are crafted from metal, thrown to light an area for a while, and repel aliens.

Phase G planned: the crawler, built and reviewed in a separate alien lab page before it enters the game.
- G1 Look. One human-sized, many-legged crawler with no eyes or face, in the same faceted low-poly style as the kit, made threatening by silhouette, posture and light.
- G2 Animation. Rigged in Blender with authored clips: idle, stalk, skitter run, rear-up shriek, lunge, grab-and-drag, recoil from light, hit, death. Exported as a GLB like the astronaut.
- G3 Behaviour. Hates light: never stands in the beam, flees flares. Stalks at the edge of the dark, circles, then rushes when the player looks away or is cornered.
- G4 Attacks. A telegraphed lunge (rears up and shrieks for a beat, then leaps); a grab that drags the player toward the dark until they break free by mashing a key; hits that short the headlamp for a few seconds.
- G5 Defence. A sidearm aimed with the mouse (few shots, slow reload, ammo crafted from metal at the colony), a dodge roll, and flares.
- G6 Lab. Model turntable, every clip on a timeline, and a test cave room where a crawler hunts a dummy astronaut. Then it moves into the caves, with more crawlers in deeper zones.

Phase H planned: crawler raids on the colony. Later: co-op, outposts and scale, Mars, more space technologies.

Current prototype day length, recipes, belt speeds, rover capacity, storage sizes, hunger rate, crop and cooking times are temporary tuning values. Realistic fuel carbon source remains unresolved. Launch fuel, research rates and robot costs are provisional too.
